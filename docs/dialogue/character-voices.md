**Status:** DRAFT - To be expanded during writing

## Writing Philosophy

### Core Principles
1. **Authentic Philadelphia Voice** - Real Philly speech patterns, no stereotypes
2. **Humor with Heart** - Funny but never mean-spirited
3. **Character-Driven** - Every line reveals personality
4. **Economy of Words** - GB limitations mean brevity is essential
5. **Subtext Matters** - What's not said is as important as what is

### Tone
- **Conversational:** People talk like real people, not NPCs
- **Self-aware:** Characters know this is absurd (moving furniture is the whole game)
- **Optimistic cynicism:** Acknowledge the struggle, find joy anyway
- **Warm:** Even difficult characters have humanity

## Barry Sharp (Player Character)

### Personality
- Earnest but inexperienced
- Wants to do good work and be respected
- Quietly observant
- Prone to internal monologue
- Growing more confident as game progresses

### Voice Characteristics
- **Early game:** Uncertain, questioning, apologetic
- **Mid game:** More confident, assertive
- **Late game:** Experienced, mentoring others
- **NG+:** Wiser, nostalgic, knowing

### Example Lines

**Early Game:**
```
"Is this how you hold a couch?"
"Sorry, sorry! First day!"
"Maybe I should ask for help..."
"How do the other movers make this look so easy?"
```

**Mid Game:**
```
"Got it. Let's move."
"I've carried heavier."
"Watch the corners, always the corners."
"This is just another Wednesday."
```

**Late Game:**
```
"Remember when this used to scare me?"
"Hey, I can show you the trick to this."
"Been doing this long enough to know when something's wrong."
"One more job, then one more after that."
```

**Internal Monologue (NG+ or reflection):**
```
"I was so nervous back then."
"That's where I met [coworker name]."
"Everything changes. Everything."
"But we moved furniture. We moved *furniture*."
```

## Coworker Characters

### Tommy "T-Bone" Barrett - The Veteran

**Personality:**
- 20 years moving furniture
- Seen everything, unfazed by anything
- Gruff exterior, soft heart
- Protects rookies
- Knows everyone in Philly

**Voice:**
- Direct, no-nonsense
- Philadelphia working-class vernacular
- Short sentences
- Occasional profanity (mild, GB-appropriate)
- Drops wisdom casually

**Example Lines:**
```
"Don't think. Lift."
"Knees, kid. Always the knees."
"That couch? I moved it in '06. Different apartment."
"You're doing fine. Better than fine."
"Coffee first. Always."
"This city'll break your back if you let it."
"But it's *our* city, yeah?"
```

### Maya Rodriguez - The Specialist

**Personality:**
- Meticulous, detail-oriented
- Studied physics, applies it to moving
- Perfectionist
- Anxious but highly capable
- Takes pride in the craft

**Voice:**
- Precise, specific terminology
- Explains things
- Questions herself, but knows she's right
- Technical without being condescending
- Enthusiastic about "proper technique"

**Example Lines:**
```
"Center of gravity is everything."
"If we angle it 15 degrees..."
"No, no - that's not how you distribute weight."
"Trust the physics."
"I've calculated the optimal path."
"Why would you do it that way when—never mind, I'll show you."
"See? Perfect."
```

### DJ (Deirdre James) - The Party Animal

**Personality:**
- Life of every party
- Boundless energy
- Deeply loyal friend
- Hides vulnerability with humor
- Knows all the best spots in Philly

**Voice:**
- Energetic, exclamatory
- Pop culture references
- Nicknames for everyone
- Stream of consciousness
- Genuine excitement

**Example Lines:**
```
"Let's GOOOO!"
"Barry! My guy! Ready to move some FURNITURE?!"
"After this job? I know a spot."
"This is heavy but have you TRIED the pierogis at—"
"Focus? I'm focused! I'm SUPER focused!"
"Best day ever. I say that every day."
"You coming to the thing? You gotta come to the thing."
```

### Marcus Chen - The Rookie

**Personality:**
- Even newer than Barry
- Nervous, eager to please
- Quick learner
- Looks up to experienced movers
- Finds wonder in small victories

**Voice:**
- Questioning, uncertain
- Over-explains nervousness
- Apologizes frequently
- Grows more confident over time
- Grateful, earnest

**Example Lines:**
```
"Am I doing this right?"
"Oh no oh no oh no—wait, I got it!"
"Sorry, I'm still learning the—"
"How do you stay so calm?!"
"Did you see that? I didn't drop it!"
"Maybe one day I'll be as good as you."
"Thanks for being patient with me."
```

### Rosa Delgado - The Boss

**Personality:**
- Runs the moving company
- Fair but demanding
- Cares about her workers
- Knows the business inside out
- Tired but persistent

**Voice:**
- Authoritative but not harsh
- Cuts through nonsense
- Dry sense of humor
- Occasionally maternal
- Business-focused

**Example Lines:**
```
"Job came in. You available?"
"Customer says the piano is on the third floor. No elevator."
"Do good work. That's all I ask."
"You break it, it comes out of your check."
"But you didn't break it. Good."
"This business'll kill you. But slowly."
"You're late." [long pause] "...Don't be late tomorrow."
```

## Customer Archetypes

### The Wealthy Tipper
**Voice:** Polite, slightly distant, generous
```
"Oh, thank you so much! Here, please, for your trouble."
"Be careful with the antique armoire, would you?"
"You boys work so hard. Girls. You all work so hard."
```

### The College Student
**Voice:** Broke, apologetic, relatable chaos
```
"Sorry about the mess. And the stairs. And... everything."
"I can help! I'll totally help!"
"Is this enough of a tip? It's all I have."
```

### The Difficult Customer
**Voice:** Demanding, critical, never satisfied
```
"That's not where I said to put it."
"Are you sure you know what you're doing?"
"I'm watching you. Carefully."
"...Fine. Here." [minimal tip]
```

### The Elderly Person
**Voice:** Kind, nostalgic, grateful
```
"You remind me of my grandson."
"I've lived here 40 years. Leaving is hard."
"Thank you, dear. You're very strong."
"Here, take this. You earned it." [good tip + cookies]
```

### The Artist
**Voice:** Eccentric, passionate, oblivious to inconvenience
```
"The sculpture is my *life*."
"Can you feel the energy in this space?"
"Be one with the furniture, man."
"I'll paint you! Immortalize this moment!"
```

## Philadelphia Voice

### Authentic Philly Characteristics
- Water = "wooder"
- "Jawn" = thing (use sparingly, not a crutch)
- Directness without rudeness
- Neighborhood pride
- Self-deprecating about Philly while fiercely defending it
- References to: hoagies, Wawa, the El, neighborhoods

### What to Avoid
- Fake tough-guy stereotypes
- Overdoing the accent in text
- "Yo" in every sentence
- Rocky references (maybe ONE)
- Cheesesteak as personality

### What to Include
- Real neighborhood names (Fishtown, Passyunk, West Philly, etc.)
- Weather complaints
- Parking complaints
- Love-hate relationship with parking authority
- SEPTA stories
- The specific way Philadelphians give directions

### Example Philadelphia Flavor

```
"Take Ridge to Hunting Park, jug handle by the Wawa, you'll see it."
"Parking authority got me again. $75 for being three minutes over."
"This is nothing. You should try moving a couch in Fishtown in August."
"Wawa run after this?"
"The El was packed. Had to wait for three trains."
"This neighborhood used to be different. Everything changes."
```

## Dialogue Formatting for GB Studio

### Technical Constraints
- Max ~32 characters per line
- Max 3 lines per dialogue box
- Text boxes appear at bottom of screen
- Player presses button to advance

### Best Practices

**Short Lines:**
```
✅ "Let's go."
✅ "You ready?"
❌ "Are you prepared to commence the furniture relocation procedure?"
```

**Break for Pacing:**
```
"We've got a big job."
[next box]
"Fifth floor. No elevator."
[next box]
"...You're gonna hate this."
```

**Show, Don't Tell:**
```
❌ "I am very tired from carrying that heavy item."
✅ [sprite: exhausted animation]
✅ "...I need a minute."
```

## Dialogue Progression

### First Job (Tutorial)
- Instructional but in-character
- T-Bone or Rosa explaining basics
- Barry asking questions
- Establishing relationships

### Mid-Game
- More banter, less instruction
- Inside jokes developing
- References to previous jobs
- Deepening character moments

### Late Game
- Callbacks to early dialogue
- Character arcs resolving
- Emotional weight
- Preparation for ending

### New Game+
- Characters are aware of cycle
- Meta-references (subtle)
- Different perspectives on same situations
- Nostalgia

## Writing Process

### For Each Character/Scene

1. **Know the purpose:**
   - What information must this convey?
   - What does it reveal about character?
   - How does it serve the story?

2. **Write long first:**
   - Get the ideas and voice down
   - Don't worry about length initially

3. **Edit ruthlessly:**
   - Cut to essential words
   - Every word must earn its place
   - Read aloud for rhythm

4. **Test in-game:**
   - See how it flows with button presses
   - Check pacing and readability
   - Adjust based on feel

## Localization Preparation

### Writing for Future Translation
- Keep idioms explainable
- Avoid puns that won't translate
- Cultural references should be clear
- Write modularly (swappable lines)

### Character Voice in Other Languages
- Document personality traits separately
- Provide context for tone
- Note important vernacular choices
- Allow translators creative freedom

## Humor Guidelines

### What Makes It Funny
- Absurdity of the situation (it's just furniture!)
- Character reactions to mundane difficulties
- Contrast between gravity and triviality
- Self-awareness
- Specific, surprising details

### Examples

**Absurdity:**
```
"We're risking our lives for a loveseat."
"Strangest Tuesday I've had. Second-strangest maybe."
```

**Character Contrast:**
```
Maya: "I've calculated the optimal—"
DJ: "YOLO!" [starts carrying piano alone]
Maya: "That's not— PHYSICS SAYS—"
```

**Specific Details:**
```
"This couch smells like 1987."
"The customer has 47 lamps. FORTY. SEVEN."
```
